--- SCENE ANALYSIS (Claude) ---
## Spatial Layout Analysis

### Overview
The scene is a 48×28×48 grid containing three objects: a terrain/castle landscape, an armored knight, and a deer. The objects occupy distinct spatial zones with clear separation.

---

### Terrain (Castle Landscape)
The terrain is the dominant mass, spanning the center of the grid (X[8..39], Z[8..39]) from ground level up to Y=27 — nearly the full height of the scene. At 7,735 voxels, it dwarfs the other objects.

In the **top view**, the interior cells with depth values `3` (around low-res cols 5–6, rows 7–8) mark the **tallest castle structures** — those depth-3 values mean surfaces are closest to the top camera, i.e., the highest elevation points. These sit roughly in the center-back of the terrain footprint, consistent with a castle on a hill.

The surrounding terrain slopes away: depth values `6–9` indicate progressively lower or more distant surfaces, forming the hillside around the castle.

---

### Knight
- Positioned **in front of and partially overlapping** the terrain's Z-front face: X[14..33], Z[5..25]
- Base at Y=8, reaching up to Y=27 — stands at the same elevation as the terrain top
- In the **front view**, the `6` (yellow) and `2` (green) values in the middle rows are the knight's sword and armor catching the view
- The knight is **west-to-center horizontally** and **forward (low Z)** relative to the terrain — essentially standing before the castle entrance

---

### Deer
- Positioned **behind and to the right** of the terrain: X[35..60], Z[35..43]
- Base at Y=8, top at Y=34 — slightly **taller** than the terrain's top (Y=27), which is notable given its `26×27×9` bounds
- In the **top view**, barely visible at the far upper-right edge (the `2` at row 2, col 11 is a lone deer voxel peeking past the grid edge)
- The deer's Z[35..43] places it behind the castle (terrain Z ends at 39), partially occluded from the front

---

### Spatial Relationships

```
         Z-BACK (Z=48)
              [DEER] — back-right, behind terrain
         [TERRAIN/CASTLE] — central elevated mass
              [KNIGHT] — front-center, facing castle
         Z-FRONT (Z=0)
```

- **Knight → Terrain**: ~3 units of separation in Z (Z=25 vs Z=8 terrain front), knight is directly facing the castle
- **Deer → Terrain**: The deer's X[35..60] extends beyond the terrain's X[8..39] boundary — it stands **to the right and behind** the castle, partially in open space past the terrain edge
- **Knight → Deer**: Maximum separation diagonally — knight is front-left (low Z, mid X), deer is back-right (high Z, high X); roughly 30+ units apart

The composition reads as a classic scene: a knight approaching a hilltop castle, with a deer in the wilderness behind and to the side.

--- EDIT PROPOSAL (Claude) ---
## Reasoning

**Moving the deer to the moat:**

The terrain's front face sits at Z=8 (high-res). The deer is currently at (35, 8, 35) — behind and to the right of the castle, partially out of the 48×28×48 bounds (X goes to 60). Moving it to approximately (22, 8, 6) places it just at the front moat area (Z=6–14), graze-able near the castle entrance, and brings it within bounds (X[22..47], Z[6..14]).

- dx = 22 − 35 = **−13**
- dz = 6 − 35 = **−29**

**Rotating the knight to face the deer:**

The knight currently stands at X[14..33], Z[5..25], facing toward the castle (roughly +Z). After the deer moves to the front-right (X≈22–47, Z≈6–14), the deer is now to the knight's **right** (+X direction). A single 90° Y-rotation swings the knight from facing the castle toward facing +X, pointing it at the relocated deer.

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- The MOVE action uses **high-res delta coordinates**, so even though the low-res view shows the deer at cell (8–9, 8–9), the actual shift must account for the full 1:1 voxel coordinate system.
- ROTATE_Y90 rotates the *entire object* around its local Y-axis — useful here because the knight's facing is encoded in its geometry, not a stored orientation vector.
- The deer was already partially out-of-bounds (X up to 60 in a 48-wide grid), so this move corrects that as a side effect.
`─────────────────────────────────────────────────`

```json
[
  {
    "action": "MOVE",
    "object": "deer",
    "dx": -13,
    "dy": 0,
    "dz": -29
  },
  {
    "action": "ROTATE_Y90",
    "object": "knight"
  }
]
```

**Result:** The deer now grazes near the front-right of the castle moat (Z≈6, X≈22–47), just where the terrain meets open ground. The knight has pivoted 90° to face toward the deer rather than the castle gate — a watchful guardian's gaze.
